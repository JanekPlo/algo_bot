# ADR-012: MVP no-go for `bghtrend_pullback` — kept as historical baseline

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-07-05
- **Project phase:** 2 (Research & Backtest MVP)
- **Authors:** Janek Płoński, Claude

## Context

`bghtrend_pullback` was chosen as the Phase 2 MVP candidate (ROADMAP §"Wybór strategii kandydatki") because it was the most developed strategy in the repo and had a coherent economic thesis (trend persistence + pullback + Xtrender momentum confirmation + ATR trailing stop). Phase 2 was structured as an eight-session pipeline (Session 1 audit → Session 2 data → Session 3 microstructure → Session 4 in-sample sweep → Sessions 5-8 walk-forward, Monte Carlo, stress, go/no-go), where the Session 4 in-sample sweep is the first gate: it decides which parameter sets are worth the expensive walk-forward pass in Session 5.

Session 4 (2026-07-04) ran that sweep and returned a **clearly negative** result. Per the kickoff's Decision 5 ("no in-sample edge → hold the WF, escalate to a pivot/refactor decision session with brain-Claude"), the sweep was stopped at 5 of 8 planned groups once the signal was unambiguous, and the walk-forward pipeline (Sessions 5-8) was suspended. A brain-Claude decision session (2026-07-04) reviewed the evidence and recommended **Option A: pivot to a new strategy candidate, formalise bghtrend as a no-go**.

This ADR is that formalisation. It is intentionally the mirror image of the "MVP go" that ROADMAP Session 8 had reserved ADR-012 for: the framework did its job and disqualified the first candidate *before* any capital, testnet time, or walk-forward compute was spent on it. That is the outcome the RBI methodology (Research → Backtest → Implement) and the walk-forward gate exist to produce.

The decision was aligned with Janek up-front (the *"PRZED implementacją"* rule from `feedback_engineering_mindset`); this session confirmed the two open questions (A1 fate of the code, A2 ROADMAP renumbering) before writing.

## Decision

**Declare `bghtrend_pullback` a no-go as the Phase 2 MVP candidate. The strategy showed no exploitable in-sample edge across the swept parameter space on BTC/ETH at 15m/1h/4h, so it does not advance to walk-forward, testnet, or live. The code is *not* removed: it stays in `algo_bot/strategies/bghtrend_pullback.py` as a historical baseline / zero-edge comparator, with a `DEPRECATED as MVP candidate per ADR-012` banner on its deep reference. Phase 2 pivots to a new candidate — a mean-reversion strategy (Bollinger Bands + RSI) — under a renumbered `MR-Session 1..N` map.**

### What "no edge" means here, concretely

The sweep evaluated 5 groups × 30 random samples = 150 configurations, each already **post-microstructure** (ADR-011 slippage + funding overlay). The WF-eligibility filter for Session 4 was `sharpe_post > 1.5 ∧ profit_factor_post > 1.5 ∧ n_trades_post > 100 ∧ max_drawdown_pct_post > -0.20`. **Zero** configurations passed (`sweep_review.json → "candidates": []`). The failure is not a near-miss against an over-strict bar: as ADR-013 records, even the relaxed pre-WF eligibility bar we adopted this session (`sharpe ≥ 1.0`) leaves the candidate set empty, and the strongest *statistically meaningful* sample (n_trades ≥ 50) tops out at `sharpe_post ≈ 0.45`.

## Evidence

Source of record: `results/experiments/sweep_review.json` (generated by `notebooks/03_bghtrend_sweep_and_walkforward.ipynb`, section 1, 2026-07-04). Five groups: `b1/BTC/1h`, `b1/ETH/1h`, `b3/BTC/15m`, `b4/BTC/4h`, `b4/ETH/4h` (b2×2 and b3/ETH were deliberately not run — the signal from the first five was already unambiguous).

**1. Candidates after the WF filter: 0 of 150.** No sample cleared even one of the four thresholds simultaneously with the others.

**2. High Sharpe appears only where there are almost no trades (statistically empty).** The top of every group is dominated by 1-3-trade samples:

| Group | top1 sharpe_post | n_trades at top1 | median sharpe | pct_positive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| b4/BTC/4h | 0.775 | **1** | NaN (too few) | 0.467 |
| b4/ETH/4h | 0.675 | **3** | 0.542 | 0.633 |
| b1/BTC/1h | 0.658 | **14** | 0.283 | 0.767 |
| b1/ETH/1h | 0.182 | 20 | -0.402 | 0.067 |
| b3/BTC/15m | 0.007 | 90 | **-1.422** | 0.033 |

In `b4/BTC/4h` the six best samples all show `sharpe_post = 0.775` on `n_trades = 1` — a single lucky trade, not an edge. The samples that *do* trade enough to be meaningful sink toward or below zero: the one high-turnover 15m sample (3131 trades) has `max_drawdown_pct_post = -1.124` (ruin), and the `b3/BTC/15m` group median Sharpe is **-1.42**.

**3. Cross-symbol failure — the edge does not transfer.** The same `b1` parameter family that looks least-bad on BTC/1h collapses on ETH/1h:

| Metric | b1/BTC/1h | b1/ETH/1h |
|---|---|---|
| median sharpe_post | **+0.283** | **-0.402** |
| pct_positive | 0.767 | 0.067 |
| top5_mean sharpe_post | 0.583 | -0.001 |

A parameter set tuned on one symbol has no reason to be trusted out-of-sample if it is negative on the sister symbol in-sample. The best BTC/1h "real-trade" sample (80 trades) reaches only `sharpe_post = 0.453, PF 1.345, DD -0.115` — the single most defensible configuration in the whole sweep, and it is still less than half the `sharpe ≥ 1.0` pre-WF bar.

**4. Microstructure is not the culprit.** The raw→post Sharpe spread is small on the higher timeframes (`b1`: 0.030-0.039; `b4`: 0.004), so the strategy is not being killed by slippage/funding — the raw edge simply is not there. (On 15m the spread mean is even slightly negative with huge variance, consistent with churn noise rather than a cost story.)

Taken together: high Sharpe is an artifact of tiny trade counts, real trade counts produce ≤ 0.45 Sharpe, and nothing transfers across symbols. This is the textbook signature of *no edge*, not of *a good edge poorly tuned*.

## Consequences

**Positive:**

- **The framework is validated as a filter.** This is the first strategy the pipeline has professionally disqualified. ADR-011 microstructure, ADR-009 walk-forward gating, the ADR-013 eligibility bar, and the Session 4 sweep heuristics together produced a defensible "stop here" *before* any walk-forward compute, testnet time, or capital was committed. That is the whole point of RBI + walk-forward.
- **A permanent zero-edge comparator.** Keeping `bghtrend_pullback` in the repo means the next candidate can be benchmarked against it ("does the new mean-reversion strategy beat a known zero-edge baseline?"), and future readers see a concrete example of what a *failed* in-sample sweep looks like in this codebase.
- **Clean pivot.** Phase 2 restarts with a fresh candidate and a renumbered session map, so the next several sessions have an unambiguous target instead of inheriting a strategy already known to be dead.

**Negative / costs:**

- **bghtrend is deprecated as an MVP candidate.** The strategy code, its four sweep configs (`bghtrend_b1..b4.yaml`), and its deep reference remain, but they no longer describe an active line of work. The deep reference carries a `DEPRECATED as MVP candidate per ADR-012` banner so nobody mistakes it for the current candidate.
- **Sunk research cost.** Sessions 1 (audit), 3 (partly), and 4 were spent on a strategy that will not ship. This is the expected cost of the R in RBI — cheaper here (backtest sweep) than in Phase 3/4 (testnet/live).
- **ROADMAP churn.** The Phase 2 session map is renumbered (see ROADMAP update), which means the original "Session 5-8" numbering is retired; the history lives in git and the captains-log, not in the current ROADMAP.

**Risks:**

- **Discarding a real-but-fragile edge.** It is conceivable that a parameter region outside the swept space, or a different symbol universe, hides an edge. Mitigated by the breadth already covered (30 samples × 5 groups spanning three timeframes and two symbols) and by the cross-symbol failure, which argues against a robust edge existing anywhere nearby. Re-opening bghtrend would require a *new* economic argument, not another parameter iteration.

## Alternatives Considered

- **Iterate parameters and re-sweep.** Rejected. The space was already sampled at n=30 across 5 groups spanning the natural timeframe bands (b3≈15m, b1/b2≈1h, b4≈4h) and both liquid symbols. The failure mode is structural (edge only at n_trades≈1; nothing transfers cross-symbol), not a tuning miss, so another iterate is high cost / low expected value. The bar for re-opening is a new thesis, not a new grid.
- **Delete `bghtrend_pullback` entirely (full deprecate).** Rejected in favour of kept-as-baseline (kickoff A1). Unlike `executor.py` (removed in the 2026-05-24 cleanup — broken import, zero consumers), bghtrend is *working code with a specific, documented negative result*; its value as a zero-edge comparator and as historical evidence that the framework filters bad strategies outweighs the cost of leaving ~480 lines in the tree. It is not on any live path.
- **Advance to walk-forward anyway "to see the decay."** Rejected. Walk-forward is expensive and answers "does an in-sample edge survive out-of-sample?" — meaningless when there is no in-sample edge to decay. Session 4 Decision 5 explicitly gated WF on a non-empty candidate set.
- **Keep Phase 2 session numbering, annotate as restarted (kickoff A2 option b).** Rejected in favour of renumbering to `MR-Session 1..N`. ROADMAP is the current-state document, not a museum; the bghtrend history is preserved in git log and `docs/captains-log/2026-07-04.md`, and renumbering gives the next sessions an unambiguous target.
